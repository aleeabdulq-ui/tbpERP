# 1. Proposal overview
## 1.1 Purpose and investment case
This proposal is submitted to the Information Service Manager of The Building Practice Limited (TBP) for coordination of management review and budget approval. It offers the implementation of TBP ERP as a shared platform for authorised personnel to manage agreed departmental processes, track work and obtain consolidated operational reports.

The proposed investment is **₦4,500,000.00 (Four Million, Five Hundred Thousand Naira Only)** for the agreed implementation and perpetual enterprise software licence. It is intended to support better accountability, reduce avoidable duplicate record entry and improve management visibility. These are intended benefits, not guaranteed financial savings or representations about TBP’s present operations.

This proposal does not assume knowledge of TBP’s salaries, payroll policies, project margins, expenditure, approval limits or statutory positions. Internal requirements will be confirmed with authorised TBP representatives before implementation is baselined.

## 1.2 Organisational coverage
The proposed coverage comprises Human Resources; Architecture and Projects; Accounts and Finance; Procurement and Store; Administration; and Information Technology, with appropriate management oversight. Departmental access and approval authority will be defined by TBP and configured within the approved scope.

The system is intended to provide a central place for authorised records, task and approval tracking, and agreed management reports. It will support TBP’s decision-making rather than replace its professional judgement, internal controls or statutory responsibilities.

## 1.3 Basis of the offer
This is a commercial budget proposal, not a statement that all functions are already complete or production-ready. Delivery requires review of the existing application, confirmation of outstanding work, an approved functional baseline and formal testing. Detailed specifications and acceptance tests will be agreed separately without changing the ten-module scope unless both parties approve a change in writing.

The commercial offer remains subject to a written implementation and licensing agreement. Budget approval alone does not resolve outstanding contractual terms or authorise additional expenditure.

# 2. Proposed functional scope
The following ten modules define the proposed scope at a business level. The list identifies intended capabilities, not TBP-specific policies or a claim of completed delivery.

| Module | Proposed business coverage |
|---|---|
| 1. Identity, security and access management | Authorised user access, role-based permissions and traceability of sensitive actions. |
| 2. Executive and departmental dashboards | Agreed operational indicators and summaries restricted to authorised users. |
| 3. Human resources and employee lifecycle | Employee records, leave, attendance, training and performance administration based on approved policies. |
| 4. Task assignment and accountability | Work assignment, deadlines, submissions, supervisory review and revision history. |
| 5. AEC project management and documentation | Project records, milestones, technical document tracking, site reports and requests for information. |
| 6. Timesheets and project cost reporting | Approved time records and project cost summaries using calculations and inputs approved by TBP’s Accounts department. |
| 7. Finance, payroll, invoicing and budgets | Agreed invoicing, payment records, payroll information, expenses, budget monitoring and financial reports. |
| 8. Procurement and inventory | Supplier records, requisitions, approvals, purchase orders, receipts and stock movements. |
| 9. Administration, assets, facilities and fleet | Asset custody, vehicle bookings, maintenance records and agreed administrative registers. |

The platform will be browser-accessible in the agreed hosting environment. Technical products and implementation choices will be recorded in separate technical documentation, not used as the basis of this budget decision.

# 3. Information and approvals required from TBP
## 3.1 Financial calculations and policies
**TBP’s Accounts department must supply, or formally approve, all financial calculation rules and relevant inputs before those functions are configured and accepted.** This includes applicable employee cost-rate methods, working-hour assumptions, salary and payroll inputs, deduction rules, project-cost classifications, budget controls and financial reporting definitions, where required by the approved scope.

No salary, hourly rate, costing divisor, tax rate, margin assumption or financial example in earlier drafts is incorporated into this revised proposal. The provider will not determine these matters on TBP’s behalf or infer them from incomplete records. Authorised Accounts representatives must validate the agreed calculations against their own approved test cases.

TBP remains responsible for the accuracy and lawful provision of its data and for approving its accounting, employment and statutory policies. The provider remains responsible for implementing approved rules correctly and correcting defects attributable to its work. Client approval does not excuse an implementation error.

## 3.2 Other departmental inputs
TBP will nominate a coordinator and authorised departmental representatives; provide approved workflows, access permissions and approval limits; supply authorised migration records in agreed formats; and arrange timely review, testing and infrastructure decisions. Only information reasonably necessary for the approved work should be shared.

Migration volumes, data formats, historical coverage and cleansing responsibilities will be agreed before import work begins. Missing information or late approvals will be recorded and their effect on delivery assessed jointly; they do not automatically authorise extra charges or unilateral changes to dates.

## 3.3 Confidentiality and controlled access
The provider will use TBP information only for authorised implementation and support purposes and restrict access to personnel who need it for that work. Confidentiality and data-processing provisions must be agreed before sensitive information is transferred. Anonymised or synthetic records should be used for demonstrations and testing wherever practicable.

# 4. Implementation and acceptance approach
| Stage | Principal activities | Completion evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Mobilisation and validation | Confirm authorised representatives, remaining work, requirements, data responsibilities and delivery plan. | Approved scope and implementation baseline. |
| Configuration and completion | Complete the approved functions, configure departmental workflows and integrate agreed reports. | Demonstrable release and internal test results. |
| Staging and user acceptance testing (UAT) | TBP representatives test workflows, permissions and Accounts-approved calculations in a non-production environment. | Written UAT acceptance and agreed minor-defect register. |
| Production and training readiness | Confirm deployment plan, authorised data, infrastructure, backup arrangements and training schedule. | Written readiness confirmation; final invoice and payment before deployment and training. |
| Deployment, training and handover | Following full payment, deploy the accepted release, conduct agreed training and deliver operating documentation. | Production verification and written production acceptance / handover record. |

**Target:** production deployment on or before **31 December 2026**, subject to a feasible implementation baseline agreed after assessment, timely mobilisation and TBP readiness. This is a proposed target, not an unconditional delivery guarantee. If the assessment shows that it cannot reasonably be achieved, the parties must agree a revised plan before a binding date is adopted.

UAT will cover all ten approved modules, critical workflows, permissions and agreed calculations. A proposed ten-business-day UAT period will be confirmed in the agreement. Material defects must be corrected and retested; minor defects may be accepted only in a written register with agreed correction dates. Silence is not acceptance.

Production acceptance will require verification of deployed workflows, demonstrated backup and restoration arrangements, completion of agreed training and delivery of user and administrator documentation. **Payment does not constitute acceptance or waive the provider’s remaining delivery and defect-correction obligations.**

# 6. Licence, warranty and optional maintenance
## 6.1 Perpetual licence and source-code rights
Upon full payment, TBP will receive a perpetual, non-exclusive enterprise licence to use the delivered application for its internal business operations. No recurring per-employee or per-department licence fee is proposed for that application. Continued use of the licensed version will not depend on renewing optional maintenance.

The licence does not automatically transfer software ownership or source code. Source-code handover, escrow or other continuity rights—including whether TBP may appoint another maintainer—must be expressly selected in the agreement before execution. Pre-existing intellectual property, third-party components and applicable licence restrictions must be identified.

## 6.2 Included warranty
A complimentary **90-calendar-day warranty starts on written production acceptance**, not on the final payment date. It covers reproducible defects in the approved delivered functions and implementation-related configuration faults attributable to the provider. New requirements, unrelated infrastructure failures and damage caused by unauthorised modifications are not included merely because they arise during the warranty period.

## 6.3 Optional annual maintenance
The proposed annual maintenance fee is **₦675,000.00 (Six Hundred and Seventy-Five Thousand Naira Only)**, activated separately after the warranty. Its defined scope may include incident support, corrective and preventive maintenance, security updates, monitoring and agreed backup checks. Support hours, response targets, exclusions, hosting responsibilities and any minor-enhancement allowance require a separately agreed service schedule. No unconditional uptime or recovery guarantee is made in this proposal.

Optional maintenance is not unlimited development or free infrastructure. Non-renewal will not terminate the paid perpetual licence or justify withholding TBP’s data. Renewal pricing and terms require written agreement.

# 7. Commercial boundaries and legal safeguards
## 7.1 Exclusions and change control
Hosting, domains, connectivity, email or SMS services, third-party subscriptions and hardware are not presumed included in the implementation price. External integrations, additional modules and work beyond the approved migration or functional baseline require prior written agreement on scope, fees, schedule and acceptance requirements. No additional paid service may be committed on TBP’s behalf without authorisation.

Defect correction within the approved scope is not additional development and will not be charged as a change request. Any proposed removal of an agreed capability also requires written approval.

## 7.2 Data protection and ownership
TBP retains ownership and control of its business data, subject to applicable law and data-subject rights. The agreement must provide accessible data-return arrangements, authorised processing instructions, confidentiality controls, retention and deletion requirements, and security-incident cooperation under applicable Nigerian data protection law, including the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023.

The provider must implement the agreed safeguards and must not sell, misuse or withhold TBP data to compel maintenance renewal. TBP must authorise access and lawful data sharing. Detailed responsibilities for hosting, backups and operational security will be allocated in the agreement.

## 7.3 Contractual protection before execution
The parties must confirm the provider’s full legal identity and authority, the contracting addresses, tax treatment, payment periods, scope baseline, delivery dates, source-code election, liability limits, any indemnities, termination and refund arrangements, dispute resolution and governing law. Nigerian law is proposed, subject to review by qualified Nigerian legal counsel.

The final agreement must address failure to complete deployment, training or handover after payment, including a cure process, termination rights and reconciliation or refund of amounts attributable to unperformed obligations. This proposal does not deem the commitment fee non-refundable or invent an agreed refund formula.

The system is an operational management tool, not a guarantee of regulatory compliance, profitability or the accuracy of information supplied by TBP. Each party remains accountable for its own obligations; no disclaimer should exclude liability that cannot lawfully be excluded.
